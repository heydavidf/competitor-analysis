# GatherSlot: fictional competitive analysis

**Synthetic example only.** GatherSlot, coordinator Mira, North Quay Swap, and all competitors and comments below are invented. This is a demonstration of the output format, not a real study or evidence of demand.

## 1. Decision context and research scope

In this simulation, Mira coordinates volunteers at a neighborhood food swap. Her problem is filling three Saturday pickup shifts without double-booking anyone. GatherSlot's proposed value is a one-screen assignment view with an availability check. The critical task is to assign those shifts and identify overlaps before publishing. Scope is fictional English-language neighborhood food swaps, rapid mode, dated 2026-09-24.

The four-candidate longlist was defined by the [invented fixtures](fixtures/). ShiftBerry and RosterCove are included as direct schedulers for the same customer and task. PatchPad is included as a generic shared-table substitute. A paper board is included as a manual substitute. No real-world search or exclusion decision occurred.

## 2. Executive summary

The simulated decision is to test whether a single-screen flow can match RosterCove's conflict warning with less setup. In these invented notes, signup is easy in ShiftBerry, RosterCove checks overlaps, and the indirect options leave checking to Mira. All grades and conclusions have **low confidence** because the evidence is fictional.

## 3. Marketplace verdict

**Purple, within this fictional scenario only.** Two invented direct options serve the task, but the invented faster flows do not flag conflicts. The gap is plausible in the fixture, not established in any real market. Absence of other competitors here says nothing about actual competition or demand.

## 4. Most threatening competitors

1. **RosterCove, direct:** its simulated conflict warning meets the central task requirement, though setup takes several screens. [Synthetic RosterCove note](fixtures/rostercove.md).
2. **ShiftBerry, direct:** its shareable signup link could attract coordinators prioritizing speed, though overlap checking is manual. [Synthetic ShiftBerry note](fixtures/shiftberry.md).
3. **PatchPad, indirect:** its flexible, low-cost table could remain sufficient for small groups that accept manual checking. [Synthetic PatchPad note](fixtures/patchpad.md).

## 5. Direct competitor findings

**ShiftBerry:** the invented coordinator creates slots, shares a link, and reviews signups. The resulting roster can contain overlapping assignments. Heuristic task grade **B**: clear entry and signup, incomplete error prevention. Its two apparent advantages are easy sharing and low entry cost; both look readily replicable in this scenario. Two invented positive comments about sharing and one invented conflict complaint are illustrative only, not a measured pattern. From ShiftBerry's perspective, its strength is simple signup, weakness is no overlap warning, opportunity is adding conflict detection, and threat is a faster conflict-safe option. [Fixture](fixtures/shiftberry.md).

**RosterCove:** the invented coordinator creates an account and recurring schedule, imports availability, assigns people, resolves a flagged overlap, and publishes. Heuristic task grade **B**: the target error is caught, with several setup screens as friction. Its warning and recurrence support are the main advantages; the former seems replicable and the latter requires more workflow work. Two invented positive comments about warnings and one invented setup complaint are illustrative only. From RosterCove's perspective, its strength is conflict checking, weakness is setup effort, opportunity is a simpler one-off flow, and threat is a lighter tool with the same warning. [Fixture](fixtures/rostercove.md).

## 6. Indirect and substitute findings

**PatchPad:** a shared table can hold the three slots, but Mira must detect overlaps. Heuristic grade **C** for this task. Its flexibility and fictional free tier are advantages; familiarity may make it hard to displace. One invented positive comment is an anecdote. From its perspective, strengths are flexibility and familiarity, weakness is manual checking, opportunity is scheduling templates, and threat is a purpose-built flow that saves coordinator effort. [Fixture](fixtures/patchpad.md).

**Paper board:** posting slots on site needs no device or account, but remote volunteers cannot see the board and Mira checks overlaps by memory. Heuristic grade **D**. Zero software cost and on-site visibility are advantages with no durable technical moat. One invented missed-shift comment is an anecdote. From this workaround's perspective, strength is on-site access, weakness is remote access and error prevention, opportunity is pairing it with a phone photo or message, and threat is a remote-friendly tool. [Fixture](fixtures/paper-board.md).

## 7. Critical-task UX benchmark and task-based comparison

All four invented alternatives are judged on the same three-shift assignment task. Grades are heuristic labels for the simulated flow, not observations of working products.

## 9. Opportunity, recommendation, and next validation step

**Refine and test:** prototype a single-screen assignment flow that visibly flags overlaps before publishing. Ask five real coordinators to assign three shifts in the prototype. Observe errors, time to complete, and whether they trust the warning; then compare against their current workflow. The key uncertainty is whether speed plus conflict detection matters enough to change behavior.

## 10. Limitations and unresolved evidence gaps

No real products, customers, reviews, prices, or markets were researched. The fixtures were invented to make a repeatable offline example. Every evidence label and confidence score in the matrix refers to this simulation; none validates GatherSlot or a real opportunity. A live run must gather dated primary product evidence, check accessible flows, look for real customer patterns, and disclose anything unobserved.
